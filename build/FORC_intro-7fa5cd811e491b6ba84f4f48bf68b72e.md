# FORC data analysis

First-order reversal curve (FORC) diagrams map the joint distribution of switching fields and interaction fields within an assemblage of magnetic particles, providing insight into domain state and magnetostatic interactions and resolving components that bulk hysteresis parameters average together ([Roberts et al., 2000](https://doi.org/10.1029/2000JB900326); [Roberts et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1002/2014RG000462)).

A FORC is a partial hysteresis curve within the interior of the major loop. The specimen is saturated, the field is lowered along the descending branch to a reversal field H<sub>a</sub>, and the magnetization is then measured at increasing applied fields H<sub>b</sub> as the field returns to saturation ({numref}`forc_explainer`a). Repeating this for successively lower reversal fields fills the loop with many curves ({numref}`forc_explainer`b) that together define a magnetization surface M(H<sub>a</sub>, H<sub>b</sub>) on the half-plane H<sub>b</sub> ≥ H<sub>a</sub> ({numref}`forc_explainer`c). The FORC distribution is the mixed second derivative of that surface ({numref}`forc_explainer`d).

## Field conventions

The notebooks and `pmagpy.forc` follow the conventions of [Pike et al. (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.370176) and the subsequent FORC literature:

- **H<sub>a</sub>** is the *reversal field* at which a curve begins (the open circle in {numref}`forc_explainer`a).
- **H<sub>b</sub>** is the *applied field* along that curve, with H<sub>b</sub> ≥ H<sub>a</sub> — the constraint that confines the data to the half-plane above the diagonal in {numref}`forc_explainer`c.
- The FORC distribution is ρ(H<sub>a</sub>, H<sub>b</sub>) = −½ ∂²M/∂H<sub>a</sub>∂H<sub>b</sub> ({numref}`forc_explainer`d).
- Diagrams are drawn in the rotated coordinates B<sub>c</sub> = (H<sub>b</sub> − H<sub>a</sub>)/2 (coercivity) and B<sub>u</sub> = (H<sub>b</sub> + H<sub>a</sub>)/2 (interaction/bias field), overlaid as arrows in {numref}`forc_explainer`d.

Fields are handled in tesla and moments in Am² throughout. Files recorded in cgs (Oe, emu) are converted when they are imported.

Be aware that MicroMag file headers use `Hb1`/`Hb2` and `Hc1`/`Hc2` for something else entirely — the display bounds of the diagram, not reversal and applied fields. The sloping right-hand edge in {numref}`forc_explainer`c is the imprint of one such bound (`Hb2`) on the measured data. [FORC_processing.ipynb](./FORC_processing.ipynb) covers how these are read.

## A note on smoothing

Because &rho; is obtained by twice differentiating measured magnetization data, smoothing is an important part of the calculation and the smoothing chosen is a consequential analytical choice in FORC processing. `pmagpy.forc` sets an initial level automatically from the geometry of the measurement and records it, with every other processing decision, in the output. The choices made for smoothing need to be documented and presented for any presented FORC diagram.

[FORC_smoothing.ipynb](./FORC_smoothing.ipynb) works through the importance of smoothing and illustrates the effect of different smoothing choices; [FORC_variforc.ipynb](./FORC_variforc.ipynb) covers the case where the data would be better treated by variable smoothing instead of a single level.

## Archiving FORC measurements

 `pmagpy.forc` enables the export of raw instrument files to a MagIC measurements table under the `LP-FORC` method code. Archiving the reversal curves themselves keeps the full processing chain including drift correction, smoothing choices, and the resulting distribution to be reproduced and revisited with different processing approaches.
