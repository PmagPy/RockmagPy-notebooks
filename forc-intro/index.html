FORC data analysis

First-order reversal curve (FORC) diagrams map the joint distribution of switching fields and interaction fields within an assemblage of magnetic particles, providing insight into domain state and magnetostatic interactions and resolving components that bulk hysteresis parameters average together (Roberts et al., 2000; Roberts et al., 2014).

A FORC is a partial hysteresis curve that samples the interior of the major loop. The specimen is saturated, the field is lowered along the descending branch to a reversal field Ha, and the magnetization is then measured at increasing applied fields Hb as the field returns to saturation (Figure 1a). Repeating this for successively lower reversal fields fills the loop with a family of curves (Figure 1b) that together define a magnetization surface M(Ha, Hb) on the half-plane Hb ≥ Ha (Figure 1c). The FORC distribution is the mixed second derivative of that surface (Figure 1d).

  • FORC Processing This notebook leads with the standard workflow — a diagram, profiles, and a ridge-following profile in three calls — and then works through what those calls do: reading raw Lake Shore (Princeton MicroMag) VSM files, drift correction, archiving to the MagIC data model, and estimation of the FORC distribution. It also covers the marginal coercivity distribution — the FORC distribution collapsed along the bias axis, which is directly comparable with backfield-derived coercivity spectra — and sets out how the implementation relates to FORCinel, VARIFORC and FORCsensei.

  • FORC Smoothing Smoothing is the consequential analytical choice in FORC processing. This notebook gives a practical recipe for choosing a level, then illustrates what the local-regression neighbourhood is, how much of it is usable near the Bc = 0 axis, what over- and under-smoothing do to a feature of known width, a residual-based selection criterion of the kind FORCinel uses, and why a single smoothing window cannot serve a narrow ridge and a broad background at once. It closes on the colour scale, which governs whether any of this is visible in the finished diagram.

  • VARIFORC variable smoothing A single smoothing window cannot resolve a narrow ridge and suppress a broad background at the same time. This notebook applies the variable-smoothing protocol of Egli (2013) to a magnetofossil-bearing carbonate carrying a central ridge, shows how the regression window varies across the diagram, uses the per-cell uncertainty of Heslop and Roberts (2012) to identify which parts of a diagram the data support, and checks the implementation against Egli’s published resolution law and against the independent FORCsensei implementation. Measurements are redistributed from the VARIFORC example datasets with attribution.

Field conventions¶

The notebooks and pmagpy.forc follow the conventions of Pike et al. (1999) and the subsequent FORC literature:

  • Ha is the reversal field at which a curve begins.

  • Hb is the applied field along that curve, with Hb ≥ Ha.

  • The FORC distribution is ρ(Ha, Hb) = −½ ∂²M/∂Ha∂Hb.

  • Diagrams are drawn in the rotated coordinates Bc = (Hb − Ha)/2 (coercivity) and Bu = (Hb + Ha)/2 (interaction/bias field).

Fields are handled in tesla and moments in A m² throughout. Files recorded in cgs (Oe, emu) are converted on read, and a file whose declared units are inconsistent with the magnitude of its data is rejected rather than silently rescaled.

Be aware that MicroMag file headers use Hb1/Hb2 and Hc1/Hc2 for something else entirely — the display bounds of the diagram, not reversal and applied fields. FORC_processing.ipynb covers how these are read.

A note on smoothing¶

Because ρ is obtained by twice differentiating measured magnetization data, smoothing is integral to its estimation rather than a cosmetic afterthought, and the level chosen is the consequential analytical choice in FORC processing. pmagpy.forc sets an initial level automatically from the geometry of the measurement and records it, with every other processing decision, in the output — but that estimate is a starting point rather than an answer. Results should be assessed across a range of levels, and the level used should be reported with any published diagram.

FORC_smoothing.ipynb works through why this is so and how to choose; FORC_variforc.ipynb covers the case where no single level will do.

Archiving FORC measurements¶

FORC data have rarely been archived at the measurement level, which matters because a published FORC diagram cannot be re-smoothed or reprocessed from the figure alone. pmagpy.forc exports raw instrument files to a MagIC measurements table under the LP-FORC method code, preserving the interleaved calibration measurements on which the drift correction depends. Archiving the reversal curves themselves keeps the full processing chain — drift correction, smoothing choices, and the resulting distribution — open to reproduction and revision as processing methods continue to develop.
